## 1. SafeDriveVLA: Navigation-Conditioned World Model Dreaming for Conflict-Aware End-to-End Autonomous Driving / SafeDriveVLA ：梦想冲突感知端到端自动驾驶的导航条件世界模型

**Date**: 2026-10-14 | **arXiv**: [2610.20609v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20609v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20609v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are gaining attention in autonomous driving, inheriting commonsense priors from large language models (LLMs) and widening the navigation interface from a fixed command set to free-form natural-language instructions. However, this open-ended interface exposes a new vulnerability surface: an inattentive driver may issue instructions that are unsafe given the current scene, and a malicious user may issue dangerous ones on purpose. We present the first benchmark suite that evaluates navigation following and conflict awareness together, along with adversarial robustness. Existing VLAs fall short on both: SimLingo completes no language-instructed lane change in closed loop, and all models degrade sharply under conflicting signals. We propose SafeDriveVLA, the first driving VLA that (1) explicitly predicts a three-level navigation-compliance mode to decouple conflict reasoning from action generation, and (2) uses navigation-conditioned world model dreaming to inform this prediction. Unlike prior world models that roll out expert or planned actions, ours dreams of strictly following the navigation signal, showing when following is safe. SafeDriveVLA reaches 88.16 Driving Score (DS) and 68.64% Success Rate (SR) on Bench2Drive, lifts closed-loop language following by 49.3% and reduces collisions under unsafe instructions from 60 to 38 compared to the best SimLingo variant. Video demonstrations are available on our project page.

## 3. PAWA: Panoramic Action-conditioned World Modeling for Multiple Agents / PAWA ：多智能体的全景动作条件世界建模

**Date**: 2026-10-14 | **arXiv**: [2610.20240v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20240v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20240v1)

**Categories**: cs.CV, cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

A multiplayer world model must predict how the independent actions of several players change a shared environment, including changes outside a player's current field of view. A point-of-view (POV) video captures such changes only after they enter its field of view, whereas panoramic video records the surroundings in every viewing direction and has served as an intermediate representation of the world state in recent world models. Learning this task therefore requires synchronized POV and panoramic videos of interacting players together with their actions, yet no existing dataset provides this combination in Minecraft, an established testbed in which players act independently in a shared, modifiable 3D world. We present PAWA, a dataset and framework for action-conditioned panoramic multiplayer world modeling. The dataset pairs each player's POV video with world-stabilized panoramic video and aligned actions. Its distributed pipeline decouples CPU gameplay capture from GPU rendering, scales beyond 64 simultaneous players, and re-renders each recorded session under new observation configurations without replaying the gameplay. On this dataset, we develop a dual-tower model that jointly predicts POV and panoramic video and couples the two streams through cross-attention that aligns tokens observing the same direction in a shared world frame. We further introduce a quadratic-phase player encoding that provides many more player codes than existing rotary codes at the same embedding dimension. Experiments show that PAWA outperforms state-of-the-art multiplayer world models in POV prediction quality while also producing high-fidelity panoramic predictions. The dataset and collection pipeline are open source to support further research on multiplayer world models.

**Categories**: cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

World models aim to learn compact representations of an environment from observations, enabling agents to predict future states and plan their behaviour. However, most existing approaches learn black-box representations, with limited ability to incorporate prior knowledge about the world. We introduce Concept-Grounded World Models (CGWMs), a neurosymbolic world model design that instead learns interpretable concept representations. We learn these concepts from observations using explicit and complete knowledge, expressed in temporal logic, that specifies the valid world states and the temporal relations governing their dynamics. **Date**: 2026-10-13 | **arXiv**: [2610.17688v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17688v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17688v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Occupancy world models provide a structured, metric representation of future driving scenes, making them a promising basis for end-to-end autonomous driving. However, existing occupancy-aware planners typically couple occupancy prediction and action generation through either hierarchical coupling or parallel multi-task learning. Hierarchical methods use occupancy either as a fully predicted future preceding action generation or as an action-conditioned rollout for trajectory evaluation, whereas parallel multi-task methods predict occupancy alongside planning through a separate branch or auxiliary objective. These interfaces retain either an explicit generation order or a role asymmetry, limiting reciprocal interaction between world prediction and action generation during trajectory formation. We propose OccWAM, an occupancy-native world-action model that jointly generates dense future occupancy and continuous ego trajectories via flow matching conditioned on scene representations. Building on this joint formulation, we introduce scene-to-ego world-action flow matching, a two-stage formulation. The first stage predicts scene-centric occupancy together with an absolute ego trajectory, providing a stable representation of future scene evolution. The same predicted world is then re-indexed into the future ego frames induced by the initial trajectory, where ego motion is represented through adjacent pose increments and a second joint flow refines ego-centric occupancy and motion for action-aligned planning. Unlike video-based world-action models, OccWAM preserves explicit semantic geometry for direct planning reasoning without pixel synthesis. Extensive experiments on Bench2Drive and nuScenes demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in closed-loop driving, open-loop planning, and future occupancy prediction.

## 26. Acting Through Registers / 通过寄存器进行操作

**Date**: 2026-10-13 | **arXiv**: [2610.17638v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17638v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17638v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

World Action Models (WAMs) use generative visual representations to condition robot actions, yet how these representations organize action-relevant information remains poorly understood. We identify persistent high-norm spatial outliers in WAM representations and find that their action relevance persists even after normalization and K/V projection attenuate their extreme magnitude. Motivated by this observation, we introduce RegisterWAM, which adds learnable non-spatial registers to the world model and uses them directly for action conditioning. Registers alone do not fully eliminate spatial outliers; pairing them with observation flow-matching (FM) loss supervision suppresses spatial outliers while concentrating high-norm responses in registers. Beyond this reorganization, observation FM loss supervision also improves action learning without registers, motivating an additional noised-observation FM objective that learns current-scene recovery from corrupted inputs. The resulting model achieves 81.64\% success on LIBERO-Plus without additional embodied pretraining data or foundation models, reaches 93.0\% average success on RoboTwin 2.0, and improves real-world manipulation performance.

## 27. From Pixels to Executable Physics: Behavior-Grounded World Models for Embodied Action Consequence Prediction / 从像素到可执行物理：基于行为的世界模型，用于具体的行动后果预测

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

In outdoor environments, varying terrain and contact conditions can cause identical action commands to produce different motion outcomes for quadruped robots.
