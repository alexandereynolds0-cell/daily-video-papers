## 1. Agentic AI for Intent Management and Control in Autonomous Communication Networks / 用于自主通信网络意图管理和控制的智能体式人工智能

**Categories**: cs.LG

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Artificial intelligence (AI)-driven automation will help future mobile networks translate high-level network intents into concrete network optimization or control actions. However, existing approaches often focus on intent management and orchestration, leaving the adaptation of underlying network optimization and control functions largely unaddressed. This article proposes a triadic agentic AI framework that co-designs intent management and intent fulfillment through three agents: an interpreter, powered by a language model, that translates and refines intents into machine-readable optimization templates; an optimizer that adapts controller policies to intent specifications; and a controller that performs fast decision-making. We discuss the design space of each agent and how the workflow can be embedded across network architectural domains. Finally, we develop a concrete realization combining a language-model interpreter with Bayesian optimization for the optimizer and multi-objective reinforcement learning for the controller, and evaluate it in a radio resource management case study for differentiated connectivity. Results demonstrate how the agentic AI framework can connect high-level intents with runtime network control, showing adaptability of intent specifications and control policies to service requirements and changing network conditions.

## 3. EmboEagle: An Efficient Agentic Embodied Framework for Long-horizon Robotic Manipulation / EmboEagle：一种用于长时域机器人操作的高效具身智能体框架

**Date**: 2026-11-23 | **arXiv**: [2611.31173v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31173v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31173v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Long-horizon robotic manipulation demands reliable execution across interdependent stages, yet standalone World-Action Models (WAMs) suffer from compounding physical errors, while stateless Vision-Language Model (VLM) supervision incurs prohibitive chunkwise latency and lacks 3D spatial persistence. We present EmboEagle, a closed-loop agentic framework that unifies subtask-conditioned WAM control, latent-space adaptive supervision, and memory-guided geometric recovery. First, we adapt sequential WAMs via decoupled two-stage flow matching that preserves cross-stage visual history while calibrating truncated action supervision at subtask boundaries. Second, without an auxiliary vision encoder, we distill teacher intervention decisions into a lightweight routing gate driven solely by the WAM Action Expert's pre- and post-chunk latent transitions and subtask embeddings. Third, upon invocation, the VLM decouples diagnostic status from discrete routing actions and executes parameterized recovery primitives grounded in structured memory (initial references, active grasp bindings, and tool traces), followed by mandatory post-repair verification. Across RMBench and LIBERO-Mem, EmboEagle achieves 91.3\% and 90.6\% average success, surpassing the strongest baselines by 8.0 and 9.8 percentage points, while boosting real-world success over VoLoAgent under identical executor weights. Moreover, adaptive gating cuts VLM calls by 74.2\% versus chunkwise monitoring (91.3\% vs. 93.7\% success).

## 5. RoboUniverse-10K: Toward the Scaling Era of Agentic Robotics via Real-to-Sim Environments / RoboUniverse-10K：通过真到虚环境迈向智能机器人扩展时代

## 8. Bridging Continuous-Time MAPF and Industrial AGV Fleet Coordination with Lazy Priority-Based Search / 使用基于懒惰优先级的搜索将连续时间多智能体路径规划（MAPF）与工业自动导引车（AGV）车队协调联系起来

**Date**: 2026-11-23 | **arXiv**: [2611.30874v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30874v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30874v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.MA

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Continuous-time multi-agent path finding on roadmaps (MAPF$_R$) offers a principled way to coordinate robot fleets with geometric collision avoidance, yet its deployment on industrial automated guided vehicle (AGV) infrastructures remains challenging, as highly connected and redundant roadmaps amplify both safe-interval reasoning and multi-agent interaction handling. To bridge this gap, we formalize AGV-MAPF$_R$-CTC, a configuration-space variant of MAPF$_R$ with precomputed continuous-time conflicts that enables the reuse of MAPF$_R$ solvers for AGV fleet coordination with minimal changes. We then revisit continuous-time priority-based search (CPBS), identifying the boundary conditions required by its safe interval path planning (SIPP) low-level planner under priority-induced constraints and enabling earlier solution detection. As a central contribution, we analyze the structure of the priority search space, clarifying which agents are affected by each priority refinement and when replanning can be avoided. <details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Cooperative perception with roadside LiDARs (RSLs) extends the sensing range of autonomous vehicles and resolves onboard-sensor occlusions, particularly at curves and intersections. Because infrastructure deployment is costly, existing work optimizes RSL configurations once, against road geometry and aggregated traffic statistics, and treats them as fixed thereafter. Traffic flow, however, is inherently dynamic, and a placement optimal on average can be far from optimal at any given moment. We introduce RSLaA (Roadside LiDARs as Agents), a framework in which each RSL adaptively adjusts its pose to the evolving traffic scene over decision-time windows. Across 10 sites and 89 six-hour traffic scenarios (890 evaluation runs), adaptive configuration consistently improves the composite perception score over static deployment. Our results indicate that RSLs could be benefitted from being treated as agents.

## 35. FunGrasp-Zero: Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Policy for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Functional Grasping / FunGrasp-Zero: Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Policy for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Functional Grasping

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Dexterous functional grasping is critical for robotic tool use, yet current dexterous hands exhibit substantial morphological diversity. This hardware heterogeneity makes cross-embodiment functional grasping a fundamental but largely unexplored problem. Existing functional grasping methods use retargeted hand poses as guidance, tying task representation to a specific embodiment and failing to explicitly capture object-centric functional contact intent, limiting generalization across hands. To address this, we propose FunGrasp-Zero, a reinforcement learning framework for zero-shot cross-embodiment functional grasping. ## 36. Distributed Interactive Conformal Prediction for Multi-Agent Systems / Distributed Interactive Conformal Prediction for Multi-Agent Systems

**Date**: 2026-11-21 | **arXiv**: [2611.29037v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29037v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29037v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO, eess.SY

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Multi-agent systems increasingly rely on learning-enabled components and distributed algorithms for perception, estimation, and control, introducing uncertainty that makes it challenging to satisfy mission requirements. Conformal prediction quantifies uncertainty with distribution-free guarantees, but existing methods do not address the challenging combination of dependence among robots, distributed calibration, and the distribution shift induced when calibrated uncertainty bounds modify the closed-loop system. We introduce Multi-Agent Interactive Conformal Prediction (MA-ICP), a distributed framework for computing robustness margins in heterogeneous systems to satisfy mission requirements with high probability. MA-ICP aggregates robot-level nonconformity scores, which measure errors in modeled or estimated quantities, into one mission-level score per robot class, allowing dependence among robots within each mission. Updating the robustness margins changes robot trajectories and distributed algorithm outputs, so we use class-dependent score sensitivities to upper bound the shift in mission-level scores. We then establish calibration-conditional coverage guarantees per robot class for the updated margins. We demonstrate MA-ICP on cooperative payload control, formation control with obstacle avoidance, and distributed target tracking missions.
