## 2. Price of Anarchy of First-Price Auctions in Agentic Markets / 代理市场中首次拍卖的无政府状态价格

**Date**: 2026-10-14 | **arXiv**: [2610.20587v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20587v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20587v1)

**Categories**: cs.GT, cs.AI, cs.LG

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

An agentic market is a decentralized serving-time market where AI agents bid to claim each user prompt. An agent's bid is based on its expected value for the prompt, which is the expectation of the reward for its response minus its inference costs. First-price auctions (FPAs), in which the highest bidder pays its bid and receives the prompt, are a natural way to allocate prompts in such a market, since they are simple and transparent to run, and do not require central knowledge of private capabilities and costs. However, FPAs are not truthful: participants may bid less than their true value in the hope of a larger profit, and the prompt may then be allocated to someone other than the bidder who values it the most. This welfare loss is quantified by the $\textit{price-of-anarchy}$ (PoA). In this paper, we argue that agentic markets are likely to possess features that reduce the price-of-anarchy. The first is a $\textit{competitive floor}$, which occurs when multiple bidders usually value the prompt above some baseline share of the optimal welfare (e.g., because the best models are often distilled into cheaper alternatives, which perform similarly). We show that competition among them then limits the welfare loss, which can be driven to zero as the floor rises. The second feature is $\textit{near-independence}$ of values after conditioning on the prompt, which occurs when most of the predictable variation in the expected value is determined by the difficulty of the prompt. As the value distribution approaches conditional independence, we can derive an efficiency guarantee that approaches a factor of $1 - 1/e^2$ of the optimal welfare. Thus, both features give rise to welfare lower bounds that are significantly better than the well-known general case of $1 - 1/e$. We validate these results empirically using auctions with strategic bidders simulated on the EmbedLLM prompt routing dataset.

代理市场是一个去中心化的服务时间市场，人工智能代理商竞标要求每个用户提示。 座席的出价基于其对提示的期望值，即对其响应的奖励减去其推断成本的期望值。 第一价拍卖（ FPA ） ，出价最高的人支付出价并收到提示，是在这样一个市场中分配提示的自然方式，因为它们运行简单透明，并且不需要对私人能力和成本的中央知识。 然而， FPA是不真实的：参与者可能出价低于其真实价值，希望获得更大的利润，然后提示可能会分配给出价者以外的其他人，而出价者最看重提示。 这种福利损失由$\ textit {price-of-anarchy} $ (PoA)量化。 在本文中，我们认为代理市场可能具有降低无政府状态价格的特征。 第一个是$\ textit {competitive floor} $ ，当多个投标人通常对提示的价值高于最佳福利的某些基线份额时（例如，因为最好的模型通常被提炼成更便宜的替代品，其表现类似） ，就会发生这种情况。 我们表明，它们之间的竞争限制了福利损失，随着底线的上升，福利损失可以降至零。 第二个特征是$\ textit {near-independence} $值在对提示进行条件调整后，当预期值的大多数可预测变化由提示的难度决定时发生。

## 3. One Task, Many Interfaces: Simple Cross-Interface Consistency Training Improves LLM Agent Robustness / 一个任务，多个接口：简单的跨接口一致性培训提高了LLM代理的稳健性

**Date**: 2026-10-14 | **arXiv**: [2610.20576v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20576v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20576v1)

**Categories**: cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Post-training binds the competence of an LLM agent to the interface it was trained on. Semantics-preserving perturbations of that interface, such as renaming an action or changing the notation, leave the underlying task unchanged yet make the agent worse at it. Scoring each sample independently means interface randomization only broadens this dependence from a single interface to the training interface distribution, so out-of-distribution perturbations still degrade the agent. We propose Cross-Interface Policy Regularization (CIPR), a self-supervised objective that replaces action names with uninformative identifiers and presents each training sample under two interfaces in which equivalent actions are identified by construction. CIPR penalizes the KL divergence between the action distributions induced under the two interfaces, so the policy behaves consistently across them. We evaluate CIPR in both supervised and reinforcement-learning post-training, where it improves robustness. On agentic function calling, held-out interface perturbations reduce the accuracy of a post-trained agent by nearly 25%, and CIPR recovers 10.5% while leaving accuracy on the unchanged interface intact.

培训后将LLM代理的能力与其培训的接口绑定在一起。 语义-保留该界面的扰动，例如重命名操作或更改符号，使底层任务保持不变，但使代理更糟糕。 对每个样本进行独立评分意味着接口随机化只会将这种依赖性从单个接口扩展到训练接口分布，因此分布外的扰动仍然会降低agent。 我们提出跨接口策略正则化（ CIPR ） ，这是一个自我监督的目标，用不具信息性的标识符替换行动名称，并在两个接口下呈现每个训练样本，其中等效行动通过构造来识别。 CIPR惩罚了两个接口下引起的动作分布之间的KL差异，因此策略在它们之间表现一致。 我们在监督和强化学习后培训中评估CIPR ，以提高鲁棒性。 在agentic函数调用时，保持接口扰动会使后训练agent的准确度降低近25% ， CIPR恢复10.5% ，同时保持接口不变的准确度。

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Unlike most verification methods, which rely on LLMs or trained models, our method combines LLM-based semantic review with mechanical numerical and vacuity checks, providing checkable feedback and detecting errors missed by LLM judges. ## 6. PerSo: Investigating Facets of LLMs' Social Intelligence in Real-World Persuasion Scenarios / PerSo ：调查法学硕士在现实世界说服场景中的社交智能方面

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Social intelligence comprises two aspects: cognitive intelligence, the ability to infer others' mental states, and behavioral intelligence, the ability to select appropriate social actions based on these inferences to achieve social goals. Persuasion provides a natural testbed in which both aspects are simultaneously at play, yet prior benchmarks typically evaluate them in isolation using synthetic scenarios. ## 7. The Wealth of Agents: Scaling Agentic AI Across Developers / 智能体的财富：跨开发人员扩展智能体AI

**Categories**: cs.AI, cs.MA

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Today's agentic systems are typically designed, built, and deployed by single developers with full control over all participating agents. While this simplifies development, it limits the potential scale of agentic systems. We study multi-developer agentic systems (MDAs) which enable developers to collaboratively build agentic systems to push frontier capability beyond what any individual developer could achieve alone. We construct empirical and theoretical models of developers to compare MDAs with single-developer agentic systems (SDAs). ## 8. Same Claim, Different Interpretations: How LLM Agents Resolve Ambiguity in Scientific Claims / 相同的声明，不同的解释： LLM专员如何解决科学声明中的歧义

**Categories**: cs.CL

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Such verifiers usually pick one, often tacitly, and benchmarks score their verdicts against expert labels without recording the expert's interpretation or the verifier's in a comparable form. ## 9. Towards Agentic Robots: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness / 走向智能机器人：模型、数据、环境和线束

**Date**: 2026-10-14 | **arXiv**: [2610.20442v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20442v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20442v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Foundation models are driving a paradigm shift in robotic intelligence, moving beyond low-level action prediction to goal-directed orchestration of data collection, model training, task planning, and real-world execution. We characterize this emerging paradigm as an agentic robots, in which agents reason over human instructions, interact with robotic environments, rectify execution errors autonomously, and dynamically adjust operational strategies. Although this field develops fast, current studies are not well connected. Researchers have no consistent view of how to combine different agentic functions into a complete robot system. This paper provides a unified overview of existing research on agentic robots. We first trace the development from robot policies to agentic systems, covering vision-language-action models, world models, and world-action models. We then clarify the definition and scope of agentic robots and organize existing research into seventeen categories across four functional domains: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness. Across these domains, we examine how agents shape model learning and robot decisions, collect and curate robot data, construct worlds, tasks, and tests for learning and evaluation, and organize capabilities for robot use. ## 58. SEAR: Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics / SEAR: Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics

**Categories**: cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CL

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Frontier language-model agents can now improve robots on their own: they write control code, test it, and keep what works as skills and memories for the next task. We introduce SEAR (Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics), a framework and benchmark built around two questions. Is the agent really learning, or just trying more often? And does what it learns still help on new tasks, in a new simulator, and on a real robot? The framework runs an agent in three modes: it writes code that becomes the policy (code-as-policy), acts live in the control loop (agent-as-policy), or works as a researcher that fine-tunes a vision-language-action model (auto-research). On a real robot, we observe a performance gap between agent-designed practice and novel human-designed scenes. Third, different models evolve more effectively in different ways. Some do best acting live, others writing code first. And a text-only model keeps pace with a comparable multimodal one: perception helps with error assessment, but agentic reasoning and tool use can matter more. We release the benchmark and codebase to support future research.

## 59. BLADE: Auditing Information Use for Failure Attribution in LLM Multi-Agent Systems / BLADE: Auditing Information Use for Failure Attribution in LLM Multi-Agent Systems

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

World action models (WAMs) built on pretrained video generation models adapt quickly to new robotic tasks, but they forget previously learned tasks under sequential fine-tuning. Experience replay (ER) mitigates such forgetting by rehearsing data of previous tasks, yet this data is often unavailable in robotics, where a policy is deployed as weights only and its training data is not shared along with it. In this work, we ask whether a WAM can achieve data-free continual learning by regenerating its own past. Our key observation is that a WAM can indeed autoregressively regenerate complete rollouts of previously learned tasks and replay them to mitigate forgetting. However, replaying such raw rollouts still lags far behind replaying real data, which we attribute to two issues. ## 62. Agentic Robot Creation through Generation, Verification, and Revision / Agentic Robot Creation through Generation, Verification, and Revision
