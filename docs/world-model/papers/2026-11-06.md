## 1. Learning a patient world model for health system simulation in intensive care / 学习重症监护医疗系统模拟的患者世界模型

**Date**: 2026-11-05 | **arXiv**: [2611.08392v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08392v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08392v1)

**Categories**: cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Designing healthcare strategies requires anticipating their clinical and operational consequences before implementation, a task that often relies on health system simulation. Such a simulation necessitates modeling how patient states evolve and generate care demands. However, conventional approaches typically tailor compact state transitions to a single policy question and omit history-dependent dynamics, thus requiring the redefinition of states and transitions when there is a new question that entails different clinical information. We propose to learn a patient world model from longitudinal EHRs using a neural network to provide a shared clinical representation of patient trajectories that can serve simulation questions across scenarios. Taking ICU operations as an example, we train the model on MIMIC-IV, freeze it, and couple its time-queryable predictions to shared ICU calendars, resources, and queues via scenario-specific mappings, so that one model renders admission demand, care tasks, and discharge proposals across simulations evaluating ICU bed capacity, care dispatch, and post-ICU transfer capacity. Experiments show that our model matches Markov and semi-Markov comparators in aggregate occupancy and directional responses to capacity and dispatch policies. It also reproduces policy gains from replayed observed trajectories. For patients entering the same support state from different prior trajectories, the learned representation captures a 24-h recovery difference (-6.4 predicted versus -6.7 observed percentage points), a contrast missed by compact state models, which propagates into distinct simulated care demand, queue burden, and bed use. These results indicate that a learned world model of patient dynamics has the potential to replace question-specific state models, allowing new care policies to be evaluated by reconfiguring the scenario without model rebuilding.

## 2. GPWM: Modeling the World as 3D Particle Motion / GPWM ：将世界建模为3D粒子运动

**Date**: 2026-11-05 | **arXiv**: [2611.08338v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08338v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08338v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

We introduce GPWM, a Generative Particle World Model that predicts how 3D scenes evolve by generating the motion of particles and rigid bodies over time. Unlike video world models that generate scene appearance, GPWM operates directly in 3D physical space. A single representation and architecture cover rigid, deformable, cloth, and granular materials, in both passive physics and robotic interaction. GPWM is an object-aware multi-resolution transformer that combines flow matching with diffusion forcing to generate coherent physical trajectories far beyond its training horizon. We train and evaluate GPWM on ParticleWorlds, a 25.5-million-frame corpus spanning diverse geometries, materials, interactions, and robot embodiments. GPWM outperforms the best prior learned-dynamics baseline in each of six physical domains, reducing prediction error by 31% on average. Breaking from the domain-specific setting of prior methods, a single Unified GPWM models these heterogeneous regimes with one set of weights, largely outperforming per-domain baselines and even surpassing our own specialist models in two domains. GPWM further generalizes to unseen geometries, physical properties, object counts, and rollout horizons. Beyond prediction, GPWM enables contact-rich planning by learning a generative prior over joint actuator-object motion. Instead of searching over unconstrained actions, planning searches the model's latent noise for interactions that achieve a desired object-space goal. With the same world model, noise-space planning substantially outperforms action-space search on pushing and picking, where complex contact sequences are hard to discover. Executed open-loop in simulation, the resulting plans achieve goal-directed manipulation without reference action trajectories or goal-specific policies.

我们引入了GPWM ，这是一种生成粒子世界模型，通过生成粒子和刚体的运动来预测3D场景如何随时间演变。 与生成场景外观的视频世界模型不同， GPWM直接在3D物理空间中运行。 单一表示和架构涵盖被动物理和机器人交互中的刚性、可变形、布料和粒状材料。 GPWM是一种对象感知多分辨率变压器，它将流量匹配与扩散强迫相结合，以生成远远超出其训练范围的相干物理轨迹。 我们在ParticleWorlds上训练和评估GPWM ，这是一个跨越不同几何形状、材料、交互和机器人实施例的2550万帧语料库。 GPWM在六个物理领域中的每个领域都优于最佳的先前学习动力学基线，平均减少了31%的预测误差。 打破先前方法的特定领域设置，单个统一GPWM使用一组权重对这些异构制度进行建模，在很大程度上优于每个域基线，甚至在两个领域超过我们自己的专业模型。 GPWM进一步概括为看不见的几何形状、物理属性、对象计数和展开水平。 除了预测之外， GPWM还通过学习关节执行器-物体运动的生成先验来实现富接触式规划。 规划不是搜索无约束的动作，而是搜索模型的潜在噪声，以寻找实现所需对象空间目标的交互。

## 3. GuidedWAM: Training-Free Energy Guidance for World Action Models in Autonomous Driving / GuidedWAM ：自动驾驶世界行动模型的无培训能源指南

**Date**: 2026-11-05 | **arXiv**: [2611.07912v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07912v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07912v1)

**Categories**: cs.AI, cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

World action models (WAMs) for autonomous driving couple the driving policy and the visual world model in a single generative network whose objectives are fixed post-training. Conditioning on a new driving objective requires retraining. We study energy guidance as a general mechanism and present GuidedWAM, a model-agnostic framework for injecting driving objectives into frozen WAMs at sampling time. Our framework constructs each objective as differentiable energies on the sampler's intermediate clean-action estimate, which needs only forward access to the frozen model, and is stabilized by sampling-time guidance scheduling, per-energy normalization, and velocity-bounded correction. On the NAVSIM v2 hard-split, guidance improves all three frozen, publicly released WAMs by 1.8 to 3.6 EPDMS, averaged over three sampler seeds, and adds less than 10% to the sampling time per step. These results position energy guidance as a practical post-training control channel for WAMs.

自动驾驶的世界行动模型（ WAM ）将驾驶政策和视觉世界模型结合在一个单一的生成网络中，其目标是固定的训练后。调整新的驾驶目标需要再培训。我们研究了作为一般机制的能量引导，并介绍了GuidedWAM ，这是一种模型不可知的框架，用于在采样时将驾驶目标注入到冻结的WAM中。我们的框架将每个目标构建为采样器中间清洁作用估计的可微分能量，该估计只需要前向访问冻结模型，并通过采样时间制导调度、每能量归一化和速度限制校正来稳定。在NAVSIM v2硬拆分上，指导将所有三个冷冻、公开发布的WAM提高了1.8至3.6 EPDMS ，平均超过三个采样器种子，并且每个步骤的采样时间增加不到10 ％。这些结果将能量引导定位为WAM的实用训练后控制通道。

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

We present PlayGen2, a real-time interactive world model that separates learned 3D state evolution from neural rendering. These capabilities demonstrate the transferability and extensibility of the learned game-world model. Code and pretrained model weights are publicly available.

## 6. Seeing Beyond the Next Step: World-Model-Guided Human-Like Navigation in Multi-Agent Scenes / 超越下一步：多Agent场景中的世界模型引导的人性化导航

**Date**: 2026-11-05 | **arXiv**: [2611.07730v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07730v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07730v1)

**Categories**: cs.GR, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Selecting a next motion in a populated scene is not just a matter of avoiding what is currently in the way: an action can be locally feasible yet drift onto the road instead of the crosswalk, cut into a stream of crossing pedestrians, or bottleneck a narrow passage. These failure modes are invisible from the current state alone, and acting on them requires reasoning about what each candidate action will cause. World models offer exactly this, namely action-conditioned predictions of the future, but rolling them out at every decision step is a cost deployable navigation systems cannot pay, and the field has largely traded foresight for speed. We argue this is a false dichotomy. The natural place for a world model in human-aware navigation is at training time, as a preference oracle that distills foresight into a fast policy, rather than at inference time, as an online planner. We pair a discrete visual action prior with two task-aligned world models, one for nearby social dynamics and one for future scene semantics, that score candidate actions by their predicted consequences and refine the policy through group-relative preference distillation. At deployment, both world models are removed and the policy acts in a single forward pass. Across five held-out evaluation scenarios, this yields navigation that is simultaneously safer, more socially and group-coherent, and more goal-directed than strong reactive and learned baselines, with naive observers rating the resulting motion as more human-like. Taken together, our findings point to a different approach for bringing world-model foresight into deployable agents: not accelerating rollouts, but amortizing them into the policy so that the cost of looking ahead is confined to training. <details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

We study whether \emph{long-horizon model-based value expansion} can serve as an effective framework in pixel-based offline reinforcement learning (RL). Long-horizon model-based value expansion from pixels is substantially harder than state-based environments, as the agent must learn both the representation and its dynamics, and errors from either can accumulate recursively over imagined trajectories. We introduce MAC-v2, which enables long-horizon model-based expansion from pixels by training a reconstruction-free latent world model with action chunking. By learning representations from prediction and control objectives, MAC-v2 does not need to encode control-irrelevant visual details that can hinder the dynamics prediction. Action chunking further mitigates the prediction error by reducing the number of recursive model predictions required for value expansion. Across diverse visual benchmarks including visual OGBench, MMBench2, Robomimic, and LIBERO-10, MAC-v2 achieves strong performance against model-free and model-based offline RL baselines. Our analyses show that long-horizon model-based value expansion is critical for the performance of MAC-v2, and that both action chunking and latent learning objectives are essential for reliable long-horizon latent imagination.
