## 3. Dynamic Task Allocation with Dialogue / 通过对话进行动态任务分配

**Date**: 2026-11-17 | **arXiv**: [2611.23119v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23119v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23119v1)

**Categories**: cs.CL

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Effective multi-agent collaboration requires agents to adapt task allocation as goals and responsibilities evolve. Prior task-allocation frameworks often assume static goals, full observability, or rely on large language models (LLMs) without explicit, verifiable beliefs about task allocation or intent. These assumptions limit their applicability to real-world collaboration, where agents often lack full information about each other's actions, progress, or local environment. To support dynamic task allocation under partial observability, we introduce a communication-augmented Bayesian task-allocation framework. Our agent uses dialogue to reduce uncertainty about the environment and collaborators, while keeping decisions grounded in explicit probabilistic beliefs. We evaluate our framework in an Overcooked domain extended with dynamically arriving tasks under both full and partial object-space observability. Our communication agent consistently outperforms a vanilla LLM agent in both settings. Additionally, our framework performs comparably to a non-communicating task-allocation baseline under full observability, but substantially outperforms it under partial observability as task complexity increases by an average speed up of 0.585 and completes on average 18 timesteps faster. These results indicate that our agent is more efficient in dynamic task allocation than baselines in partially observable environments, and that communication is important as available information decreases.

## 4. PhysScope: Agentic Multimodal Physics Reasoning with Grounded Visual Memory / PhysScope：基于扎实视觉记忆的代理式多模态物理推理

**Categories**: cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Vision-language models (VLMs) can correctly interpret physics diagrams yet apply constraints outside the states or conditions in which they hold, causing premise errors to propagate through subsequent calculations. We introduce PhysScope, an inference-time visual harness that supports self-correction of these scope mismatches without updating model weights. PhysScope enables the model to link physical scopes directly to its visual observations and explicitly constructs derived views to support reasoning. The harness imposes explicit constraints on state distinctions, conditions, and physical scopes during model reasoning, and checks agent-proposed revisions against sources and scopes. Accepted revisions invalidate registered dependents, thereby maintaining scope-aware working memory. On PhysAlign, PhysScope achieves higher solving scores than direct solving across all six evaluated VLMs. With Gemini-3.8-flash, it outperforms Canvas-CoT by 9.34 percentage points and substantially improves solving accuracy on the out-of-distribution (OOD) PhysUniBench benchmark. Ablation studies support explicit scope management and dependency maintenance as design principles for multimodal physics reasoning agents.

## 42. Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB: An Open and Efficient Small Language Model for Fast Conversational Agents / Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB: An Open and Efficient Small Language Model for Fast Conversational Agents

## 43. Instant Weight-Space Consolidation for Long-Term Memory in LLMs / Instant Weight-Space Consolidation for Long-Term Memory in LLMs

**Date**: 2026-11-16 | **arXiv**: [2611.20759v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20759v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20759v1)

**Categories**: cs.CL

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Agentic memory systems give LLMs persistent memory by extracting experience into text and retrieving it at query time, but the model itself does not evolve: however many interactions it has had, it starts each query the same. Extraction errors compound, implicit cross-session patterns that no single fragment states cannot be surfaced, and the construction cost grows with every conversation. Fine-tuning on past interactions does change the weights, but requires backpropagation at serving time and degrades on long histories. We introduce CLAM (Carried Latent Adapter Memory), which combines the strengths of both: a recurrent hypernetwork performs parametric consolidation, compiling a stream of session activations into a low-rank adapter in a single forward pass with no gradient steps and no language-model generation calls, while a complementary episodic buffer supplies verbatim detail at inference. A gated latent state carries knowledge across sessions in fixed size, so the parametric memory accumulates without growing. On LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S across three open models (2-7B parameters), CLAM leads all agentic baselines by 1-45 points and all gradient-based parametric methods by 10-45 points, while requiring zero generative LLM calls, zero gradient steps, and 3-151x less wall-clock time than extraction systems to build its memory. The two channels are complementary: the consolidated adapter adds 12-15 accuracy points when the episodic buffer misses the gold evidence, and still adds 4-5 points even with an oracle buffer.

## 44. Offline-First Quantized AI: Design and Evaluation of an On-Device Maternal-Health Assistant for Low-Connectivity Environments / Offline-First Quantized AI: Design and Evaluation of an On-Device Maternal-Health Assistant for Low-Connectivity Environments

**Date**: 2026-11-15 | **arXiv**: [2611.20012v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20012v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20012v1)

**Categories**: cs.CL

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Offline language models could extend access to maternal-health information where internet connectivity is unreliable, but deployment requires both clinically appropriate responses and feasible local execution. This study presents an offline Android assistant integrating ready-made quantized language models, a clinically validated local knowledge package, conversational context, and model-initiated local retrieval. Gemma 4 E2B and Qwen3.5 0.8B were compared across 45 conversations and 312 turns per configuration, including a dedicated offline agentic benchmark. Gemma achieved higher response-quality, context-retention, and safety scores; Qwen offered faster generation and lower memory use. Clinician assessment of 150 Gemma responses across nine clinical domains yielded a mean quality score of 3.80/4, with 147 responses meeting the acceptance criterion. A paired RAG-on/off experiment evaluated 74 questions: retrieval improved 11 responses, left 56 neutral, produced four adverse response changes, and left three pairs unassessable. RAG-on had descriptively higher mean usefulness, safety, correctness, and actionability in the conditional 70-pair subset excluding the four flagged pairs from both conditions. Physical-device testing demonstrated offline responses on a 6 GB Android handset, with a 14.3 s median latency, alongside successful reconnection synchronization in the integrated application. Together, the findings document clinical response quality and practical performance of the integrated offline maternal-health assistant.
