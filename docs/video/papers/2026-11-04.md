## 2. Diffusion Plug-in Priors for Self-Supervised Ultrasound Video Restoration / 用于自我监督超声视频修复的扩散插件先验

**Date**: 2026-11-03 | **arXiv**: [2611.05206v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05206v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05206v1)

**Categories**: cs.CV

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

\textit{Objective:} Ultrasound videos are limited by hardware-constrained spatial resolution and signal-dependent speckle, while paired low- and high-resolution acquisitions for supervised restoration are rarely available. We aim to jointly super-resolve and despeckle ultrasound videos without paired training data or network retraining. \textit{Methods:} We propose UltraDiff, which uses a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (DM) as a plug-in prior and restores each frame by optimizing the latent seed of the DM sampler under a range-constrained maximum a posteriori formulation. We derive a speckle-aware data-fidelity term from a Rayleigh scale-family model and characterize its relative-residual and variance-adaptive weighting properties. We further introduce a stabilized fidelity that is bounded below and strictly convex in log intensity for positive observations in its unclamped form. A total-variation regularizer complements the DM prior, and a temporal seed update warm-starts each frame from the optimized seed of its predecessor. \textit{Results:} On EchoNet-LVH videos with simulated speckle and additive noise, UltraDiff with a natural-image prior improves PSNR by 1.24\,dB over the strongest baseline, and ultrasound-related priors improve it further. On real clinical echocardiography videos, UltraDiff reduces the mean absolute error of zero-shot ejection fraction (EF) prediction from 7.31 to 6.37 EF points relative to DUP, the strongest baseline. \textit{Conclusion:} Combining a speckle-aware fidelity with frozen diffusion priors enables ground-truth-free ultrasound video restoration that benefits downstream measurement. \textit{Significance:} UltraDiff needs neither paired data nor retraining and is not tied to a specific diffusion checkpoint. Code associated with this work will be made publicly available upon acceptance.

## 3. RoboTryOn: Synthesizing Dexterous Robot Demonstrations from Human Videos via Virtual Try-On / RoboTryOn ：通过虚拟试用从人类视频合成灵巧机器人演示

**Date**: 2026-11-03 | **arXiv**: [2611.05163v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05163v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05163v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO, cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Robot foundation models have shown remarkable performance in robot control, yet adapting them to a new task still relies heavily on teleoperated demonstrations. Egocentric human videos offer a scalable substitute, but overlaying rendered robots on human videos or retargeting human poses into robot actions leaves significant visual or action gaps for high-DoF robot embodiments. To address this limitation, we propose RoboTryOn, which treats human-to-robot translation as virtual try-on: try-on keeps the person and swaps the garment, RoboTryOn keeps the demonstrated motion and swaps the human for the robot. Specifically, RoboTryOn converts a human video into a video of the target robot specified by a reference image, while preserving the original task, objects, and background. To maintain visual consistency, we translate selected keyframes autoregressively and use them to guide video synthesis with motion conditioned on the human hand skeleton. Additionally, we use an inverse dynamics model (IDM) that incorporates depth features to infer action labels from synthesized videos for policy learning. Our experiments across multiple humanoid embodiments show that policies trained with our synthetic data improve the average success rate on novel tasks, including challenging bimanual manipulation, from 2% to 60%, without any real robot demonstration of those tasks. Moreover, training with our synthetic data matches the performance of policies trained solely on teleoperated demonstrations at approximately a third of the collection cost, demonstrating the cost efficiency of our approach.

## 4. FloMo: 3D Scene Flow as a World Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video / FloMo ： 3D场景流作为从人类视频中学习的世界行动模型中间体

**Date**: 2026-11-03 | **arXiv**: [2611.05062v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05062v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05062v1)

**Categories**: cs.CV, cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Pretrained video generation models distill internet-scale motion priors into robot policies, but full video is a redundant signal for action prediction. We argue that dense 3D motion is the medium through which video pretraining best transfers to robot actions, and that predicting motion rather than video affords better sample-efficiency for action prediction. FloMo is a 5B video-generation model fine-tuned to predict 3D scene-flow and action tokens. Rendering scene flow as RGB and encoding it into the same latent space as video enables a pretrained video generation backbone to efficiently adapt to predict 3D scene flow. Co-trained on action-free human video and action-labeled robot teleoperation data, our model benefits from human-video pretraining and generalizes to unseen objects, motion primitives, and tasks. Replacing motion prediction with a video prediction objective degrades out-of-distribution generalization, while adding a video prediction target alongside motion lowers policy success rather than improving it. When motion, video, and action are predicted jointly, action tokens attend far more to motion than to video, suggesting that predicting video wastes model capacity. **Categories**: cs.CV

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs) have achieved strong performance on many video understanding tasks, yet remain vulnerable to temporal hallucination in time-sensitive reasoning scenarios such as scene transition understanding and event ordering. Existing solutions either require costly retraining or introduce inefficient multi-pass inference and external supervision at test time. In this work, we show that temporal evidence is already encoded in Video-LLM hidden representations but is not fully exploited during standard inference. Motivated by this observation, we propose Single-Pass Orthogonal Decomposition (SPOD), a training-free framework that recovers suppressed temporal evidence through analytical hidden-state manipulation in a single forward pass. SPOD consists of two components: Frame Distinctiveness Analysis (FDA), which identifies salient frames from middle-layer hidden-state geometry, and Temporal Delta Subspace (TDS), which constructs orthogonal temporal and prior subspaces from weighted inter-frame hidden-state differences. ## 8. Future Representation Distillation for Imagination-Free World Action Models / 未来无想象力世界行动模型的代表性蒸馏

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

World Action Models (WAMs) use the predictive knowledge of pretrained video diffusion models for robot control through future video prediction. However, iterative video denoising is costly, and inaccurate visual predictions can compromise action prediction. Our key idea is to make this predictive knowledge available through visual representations computed from the current observation, enabling action prediction without generating future videos. We introduce DreamFree, a WAM that repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as a predictive visual encoder through representation distillation. The visual encoder learns to predict a frozen video diffusion model's representation using only the current observation. An action module is then trained to predict actions by extracting action-relevant information from the visual encoder. ## 19. W2A: Distilling World Priors into Action Models for Real-Time Robotic Control / W2A ：将世界先验提炼成实时机器人控制的行动模型

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

World Action Models (WAMs) have recently shown great promise in robotic control, benefiting from the future imagination capability of large-scale pretrained video models. However, most existing WAMs require explicitly imagining future states at test time, incurring substantial computational overhead and latency that make them unsuitable for real-time robotic control. In this paper, we propose W2A, a two-stage distillation framework for real-time robotic manipulation that distills intermediate denoising representations from a video diffusion model into an action model during training, while entirely discarding the video model at test time. We hypothesize that although video latent representations are high-dimensional, a compressed low-dimensional subspace is sufficient to provide effective supervision for action generation. This makes it possible to transfer such information effectively into an action model. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings demonstrate competitive success rates while significantly improving inference efficiency. On LIBERO, W2A achieves a 95.9% average success rate. More importantly, W2A achieves about 21x faster inference than the representative WAM baseline.
